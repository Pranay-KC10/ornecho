Coverage data has blind spots

Carriers, campuses and network planners decide where to build from data that is biased, inconsistent or out of date.

01

Crowdsourced data

Only devices that already have a connection can report back. The places with the worst coverage are the least likely to appear in the data, so maps look better than reality.

02

Manual surveys

Antenna height, tilt and even the technician's own body change the reading, and more so at higher frequencies. Two crews surveying the same site can come back with different answers.

03

Predictive models

Simulation fills the gaps between measurements, but it can't see a new building, seasonal foliage or terrain that has changed since the last survey.

Mission profile

From survey volume to 3D map

  1. 01

    Plan

    Define the survey volume in the ground station app. The planner splits it into altitude layers and generates a boustrophedon (lawn-mower) path for each one.

  2. 02

    Launch

    RS-1 lifts off vertically on four lift motors, transitions to wing-borne flight at 30 m and climbs as a plane to the top layer. No runway, no launcher.

  3. 03

    Survey

    It flies the top layer first, then glides down to each layer below. A mmWave radar watches for obstacles while the RF payload samples and geotags signal strength.

  4. 04

    Deliver

    RS-1 returns home, lands vertically and offloads its log over WiFi. The ground station builds a 3D coverage map you can slice by altitude and export.

A swept flying wing carries four lift rotors on carbon booms for vertical takeoff and landing. In cruise, the lift motors shut down and a single rear pusher does the work, which is what makes hour-scale endurance possible. Hover-to-cruise transitions are handled by ArduPilot QuadPlane.

Technology

Engineered in-house

The subsystems are split across separate boards so each one can be serviced, upgraded or swapped on its own.

Avionics

Power conversion, the central control unit (CCU) and the flight controller share one main board. The CCU takes mission commands from the ground link, translates them to MAVLink for the flight controller, and coordinates the radar and RF payload over a CAN bus.

  • Dual STM32H743 processors: one for the CCU, one for the flight controller
  • IMU, magnetometer, barometer and GPS fused by an Extended Kalman Filter
  • Protected buck power stage feeding the 5 V and 3.3 V rails

RF payload

The RF payload is a self-contained daughter board. It samples received power across the 2.4–2.5 GHz band, averages a burst of readings at each point, geotags the result and writes it to local storage. To measure a different band, swap the board.

  • Antenna, band-pass filter and RF power detector on a 50 Ω controlled-impedance path
  • RF section simulated in AWR Microwave Office and Ansys HFSS before fabrication
  • WiFi stays off for the whole survey, so the payload never measures its own transmitter

Obstacle radar

TI IWR6843 FMCW radar at 60–64 GHz, the band the FCC allows for radar on unmanned aircraft. Results go to the CCU over CAN.

Dual-link comms

Commands and telemetry over a 915 MHz ExpressLRS link with 4–7 ms latency. Bulk survey data comes back over WiFi after landing.

Data integrity

Compact binary logs with a checksum on every record. Sequence IDs make WiFi transfers resumable if the link drops.

Mission software

The ground station plans the coverage path, monitors the flight live and builds the final 3D map.

System architecture

Light on the aircraft. Heavy lifting on the ground.

Path planning and data processing run on the ground station, which keeps the aircraft's electronics small and power-efficient. Flight-critical decisions such as stabilisation and failsafes stay on board for fast response.

Applications
